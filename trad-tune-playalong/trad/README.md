# Trad pack (Irish and other session tunes)

Extra guidance on top of the main [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Use it for session tunes and trad songs.

## Chord sources
- https://www.vashonceltictunes.net/irish/
- https://thesession.org/

Verify the melody matches the supplied sheet, not just the title. Settings differ in key, repeats and bar count. Source chords are a comparison, not the only right answer.

## Mandolin
- Standard G-D-A-E tuning. Show the current chord diagram and the tune's chord shapes.
- Check each voicing produces the named chord, with string order and fret numbers clear.

## Bodhran (optional)
- A restrained session-style pattern with fills at part ends.
- Toggling it must preserve playback position and stay in sync with the cursor.
- Keep bodhran clearly audible in the complete mix, with enough energy to hear the rhythm against the melody; guitar stays behind the melody. Check by ear as well as meters when possible. A meter ratio is a project target, not a trad standard.

## Guitar accompaniment
- Jigs: down-up-down / down-up-down in 6/8, with accents on subdivisions 1 and 4. Keep six subdivisions per bar distinct from the two dotted-quarter beats.
- Reels: eighth-note motion, bass pulses and accented downstrokes. Keep chord changes aligned to the printed bars and repeats.
- Do not apply jig/reel patterns to polkas or waltzes; follow each tune's meter and phrasing.

## Beginner tin-whistle support
- Preserve or add letter names under sheet notes, without covering notation. Match octave and accidentals to the actual score; the key signature still matters.
- First identify the whistle's key: cover all six holes, blow gently and match the lowest note to a piano or tuner, allowing for octave. A D whistle is named for that lowest D, not for the key of every tune it can play.
- A D whistle's basic ascending scale is D, E, F#, G, A, B, C#, high D. Teach covering holes fully, left hand above right, light low-note breath and stronger controlled upper-octave breath. Do not teach piano readers to ignore accidentals or key signatures.
- Offer an optional fingering chart with top-to-bottom covered/open holes, low and high registers, C natural for G-major tunes, and each tune's actual note range. Verify the chart against a reliable maker/teacher source and the user's whistle; C-natural cross-fingerings and upper-register variants can differ. Do not print an unverified cross-fingering as universal.
- Pick one simple phrase from the user's actual sheet and show its notes, finger changes and breath before adding UI. Check the tune fits the whistle's range. Muting the melody part lets the user play it; other parts remain in sync.
- Other instruments can play the same written concert pitches when their range permits. A recorder in C is not automatically incompatible with D/G-key backing tracks; key name alone does not imply transposition.

## Style
- Irish session look: cream, deep green, restrained gold accents, serif titles.
- Reels and jigs count beats differently. Say whether the tempo is quarter notes or dotted quarters.
- Session pace is roughly 1.5x for reels and 1.75x for jigs of a slow practice tempo. Offer speed steps up to that.
